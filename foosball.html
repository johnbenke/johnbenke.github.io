Foosball Table — CAD & GD&T Tolerancing

MEMS 202 Computer-Aided Design · Team project · Spring 2024

A full CAD exercise taken all the way to a released drawing package: a complete foosball table assembly of 8 unique part types across 22 components, with GD&T applied to every rotating and sliding interface using standard ANSI free-running fit tables. The decisions that mattered most were about what not to build — a tube-based ball-return system and corner ramps were deliberately scoped out in favour of a simpler, cheaper design that met the same functional requirements.

  • 22components
  • 8unique part types
  • 1.000″–1.002″bore vs. 0.9953″–0.9965″ shaft

Design process

  • Defined functional requirements — unobstructed pole rotation, functional ball return, integrated scoring — before any geometry was modeled.
  • Evaluated material trade-offs against those requirements, selecting stainless steel poles for load-bearing strength against polyethylene players for cost-effective replaceability.
  • Applied design for manufacturability by rejecting a tube-based ball-return system and corner ramps, removing complexity that added cost without adding function.
  • Performed GD&T calculations using standard ANSI free-running fit tables for every rotating and sliding interface, holding the pole-to-table bore at 1.000″–1.002″ against a 0.9953″–0.9965″ shaft.

My contribution

  • Designed the player-model geometry, led final assembly integration of all team-modeled components, and authored the background, design-process and outcomes sections of the technical report.

Deliverables

  • A complete multi-sheet released drawing package: assembly drawing with section views and integrated bill of materials, plus individual part drawings with full dimensioning, GD&T callouts, fillet and round specifications, and formal title blocks.
