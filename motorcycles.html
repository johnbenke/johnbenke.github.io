Motorcycle Restoration & Modification

1994 Honda Elite SR50 & 1995 Honda XR200 · Personal project · February 2025 – present

Two non-running Hondas returned to operational condition. Both were self-directed with no manual walking me through the failure, so every problem started as a symptom and had to be narrowed to a cause before any part was ordered. The engineering value here is the diagnostic method rather than the wrenching: isolating internal engine wear on the XR200 to worn piston rings, instead of replacing components until something worked, is the piece of this project I would most want to talk through in an interview.

Diagnostic process

  • Worked each fault backward from symptom to cause — compression and performance loss on the XR200 traced to worn piston rings; inconsistent running on both vehicles traced to carburetor contamination and calibration rather than ignition.
  • Validated every repair through iterative test cycles before moving to the next system, isolating one variable at a time so a fix could be confirmed as the fix.
  • Evaluated OEM against aftermarket components for fit, material and performance compatibility before committing to a part.

Honda Elite SR50 — an incomplete bike made whole

  • The scooter arrived incomplete, so the first job was working out what was missing and sourcing it before anything could run at all.
  • Fitted a new piston and cylinder, then re-jetted the carburetor to match the engine's new displacement — more air needs more fuel, and a big-bore kit with the original jetting runs lean.
  • Wired in an RPM instrument, tracing the stock harness for a usable signal rather than cutting into anything that had to stay original.
  • Fitted new tires and suspension components.
  • It is now my daily transport — I ride it to class every day.

Honda XR200 — diagnosing a smoking four-stroke

  • The bike came to me as a smoking four-stroke. Smoke means oil is getting somewhere it should not, but it does not tell you where, so the job was narrowing it down.
  • Ran compression tests, then pulled the head and cylinder apart to find where the wear actually was — measuring the engine rather than guessing at it, and confirming worn piston rings as the cause.
  • Refreshed the front shock seals, which were leaking suspension oil.
  • A lot of hardware was simply missing. Each fastener had to be researched or test-fitted to find a correct replacement, across the whole bike.
  • It now runs at dirt bike parks without failure.

Across both

  • Fabricated and installed a new exhaust system.
  • Overhauled the braking systems end to end, and cleaned and recalibrated carburetors to resolve fuel delivery failures.
  • Evaluated OEM against aftermarket parts for fit, material and performance before committing to anything.

What I took from it

Both bikes came back to life in a college garage, which meant every repair was a question of diagnosis and sourcing before it was ever a question of turning wrenches. I had no manual walking me through either failure, so each one started as a symptom I had to narrow to a cause — and the compression test on the XR200 is the piece I would most want to talk through, because it turned "it smokes" into a measurement and a specific worn part.
